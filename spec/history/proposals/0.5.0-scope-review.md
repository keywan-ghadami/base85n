# Review of the 0.5.0 scope document

The scope document for 0.5.0 asked for an independent check before
implementation, "in particular of the bit budgets in 1.1 and the estimate in
1.5", and left the measurements of its section 4 open. This is that check,
against the implementation that shipped. Everything below is measured on the
13-file, 6.52 MB benchmark corpus unless it says otherwise.

**Summary: the scope holds. Two of its numbers do not.** The bit budget in 1.1
does not close as stated, and the estimate in 1.5 is out by a factor of three —
in the proposal's favour. One design decision came out the opposite way from
the document's reasoning, and is explained under 1.2.

## 1.1 — the bit budget does not close with a type bit

The document proposes one Fill mode with two variants "distinguished by a type
bit", 20 bits for variant A and 22 for variant B. A fixed-width type field
makes the range 2<sup>23</sup> = 8,388,608 values wide. Above Dynamic
Passthrough there are 7,868,101. **It does not fit.**

Two adjacent sub-ranges do: 2<sup>19</sup> for the solid variant and
2<sup>22</sup> for the tail variant is 4,718,592 values, and one comparison
separates them exactly as a type bit would have. That is what is implemented,
and it has the side benefit that the solid variant's encoding is unchanged from
0.4.0.

The claim that "around 500,000 signal values come back" from the merge is not
reproducible and appears to be an error: the solid variant uses all
2<sup>19</sup> of its values and the tail variant all 2<sup>22</sup> of its own.
What is true is the total left over — **3,149,509 values** remain as
`FUTURE_SIGNAL_SPACE`, down from 7,343,813.

The document's reasoning for *why* variant B fixes the byte value at `0x00` is
correct and worth keeping: an arbitrary value plus two literals is 8 + 16 bits
before any length field, and 22 are available. The zero is the price, not the
feature.

## 1.5 — direct length encoding, and why the estimate was low

The document's arithmetic: at `L = 4m−2`, 0 to 3 zeros per run fall back into
block mode, roughly 22,600 triggers × 1.5 wasted bytes ≈ 42,000 characters
≈ 3.9 % of the ELF, landing it at ~1.033.

Measured, encoding the length directly takes `_cffi_backend.so` from 1.12618 to
**0.96541** — 14.3 %, not 3.9 %, and past Ascii85's 1.026 rather than up to
within 1 % of it. Over the corpus the same change is worth 2.7 % where the
step-of-four form was worth 0.9 %.

The estimate is low because it counts only the wasted zeros. It does not count
the triggers the step-of-four form never gets: a run of 3, 4 or 5 zeros plus
its two neighbours is 5 to 7 bytes, and a signal that must consume a multiple
of four either takes 4 (which block mode also does in five characters, so no
gain) or 8 (which needs bytes that are not there). Direct length turns those
into gains instead of no-ops, and short runs are the common case — the ELF has
57,762 runs of at least 3 zeros with two bytes to follow, against 19,775 of at
least 6.

**Field width.** The document offers 5 bits with the order bit or 6 without.
Measured: 5 + order gives 1.00698 over the corpus, 6 without gives 1.00889.
The order bit is worth more than the extra length bit, so it stays. Neither
choice is close to the 8-bit length the earlier draft wanted, and capping the
tail variant at 32 zeros costs nothing measurable, because runs longer than
that are the solid variant's business.

## 1.2 — the lookahead, which came out the other way

The document's diagnosis is right: with the order bit, an encoder that looks
ahead and one that does not produce different output, and that is a canonicity
violation rather than an implementation choice. Its resolution — make the
cheap, no-lookahead variant the norm — does not survive contact with 1.5.

With the length encoded directly, the smallest tail candidate is 3 zeros. Three
zeros are not 5 identical bytes, so the *existing* Fill test in the skip does
not see them: under the step-of-four form the order-0 case was covered for free
because it needed 6 zeros. Both orders now need the skip to test for them, so
there is no "cheap variant" left to make normative — the skip has to be exact
in both, or the encoder is not conforming.

Spec section 6.5 rule 6 states that directly (the positions the main loop
visits are part of the canonical form), and 11.1 states that a skip may stop
early but may never pass over a position where a construct applies.

The cost the document feared did not appear. Both orders need a zero at
`position + 2`, so one load gates both scans, and splitting the skip loop where
the whole test window is known to be in bounds removes three bound checks per
four bytes. Measured against 0.4.0, C at `-O2`, callgrind: encode is **0.97×**
on random input — faster than the version with no tail variant in it at all —
1.00× on text, 0.99× on mixed, 0.97× on the tar. The ELF pays 1.40×, where the
signal fires 60,000 times and buys 14 % smaller output. Decode is 1.00×
everywhere.

## 1.4 — `MIN_FILL_IN_SEGMENT_BYTES`, four axes as asked

The document asked for candidates 8/11/16/24/32 measured on four axes, and
guessed the answer is above 11. It is: **16**.

| threshold | corpus ratio | DP bytes | DP segments | `countries.json` decode | encode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 0.99696 | 3,160,919 | 22,354 | 482 MB/s | 158 MB/s |
| 13 | 1.00661 | 3,522,974 | 18,789 | — | — |
| **16** | **1.00698** | **3,531,218** | **18,248** | **643 MB/s** | **198 MB/s** |
| 18 | 1.02058 | 3,770,831 | 6,224 | — | — |
| 24 | 1.02264 | 3,796,277 | 5,172 | — | — |

Ratio is a step function, not a slope: 11 and 12 are one plateau, 13 to 16 the
next, 18 upward a third. 16 is the top of the second, which is where the other
three axes are best within the same ratio. The document's warning against
fitting a constant to 13 files is why the plateau edge was chosen rather than
the optimum.

## 1.3 and 4.4 — convergence, and a distance 30× the expectation

Implemented as described, and it works: `encode_parallel` produces the
sequential encoder's output byte for byte at every thread count, and reaches
2.70× on four cores and 3.40× on eight (4-core machine, 16 MiB).

The expectation in 4.4 — "a few hundred bytes in text, considerably shorter for
binary files, because block mode converges after at most 4 bytes" — is wrong,
and wrong in the direction that matters. Block mode does not converge after
four bytes: two encoders inside a block-mode run are four-byte-out-of-phase and
stay that way until some construct realigns them. Measured at 3,900 random
boundaries: median 1,078 bytes, mean 9,892, 95th percentile 50,604, maximum
226,279. `bootstrap.css` — one long passthrough stream — has a median of
21,497, and `_cffi_backend.so` a mean of 32,377.

The consequence is a floor on chunk size, not a defect: chunks are a megabyte
at minimum, and anything smaller is encoded on the calling thread.

Writing the parallel encoder also found a real defect in the sequential one.
Bounding the workers' skip by handing `next_decision_point` a truncated slice
made its own tests fail near the bound, so a worker could skip *over* a decision
point instead of merely stopping early at one. The bound now limits which
positions are tested, with the tests still reading the whole input.

## 4.2 — the minimum run length

Swept over 1 to 6. At `N = 1` the signal is a loss (3 bytes for 5 characters
against block mode's 3.75). At `N = 2` it is exactly neutral — 4 bytes for 5
characters, which is block mode — so 2 and 3 give identical corpus totals and
differ only in how many signals they emit (79,393 against 57,577). **3** is the
smallest strictly-gaining value and is what shipped.

## 4.5 — the short space runs, documented rather than fixed

With the in-segment break at 16, runs of 4 to 15 spaces ride through Dynamic
Passthrough at one character per byte: **53,673 runs, 448,321 bytes, 6.9 % of
the corpus.** `countries.json` accounts for 27,092 of them and `lodash.js` for
15,346. Spec section 14.4 records it as a known gap with the reason it is left
open — closing it needs a construct that resumes passthrough without a fresh
signal, which puts state across a signal boundary and costs the property the
parallel encoder is built on.

## Where the document's own numbers landed

| claim in the document | measured |
|---|---|
| corpus "about 1.015" | 1.00698 |
| ELF "~1.033 instead of 1.073" | 0.96541 |
| Python module at 0.962 | 0.97256 (the threshold change gives some back) |
| Ascii85 corpus 1.188 | 1.1881 |
| Alignment costs +2.0 % | +2.0 % (unchanged from the earlier evaluation) |
| `MIN_FILL_IN_SEGMENT_BYTES` above 11 | 16 |
| tail variant helps one corpus row | five rows change, one of them by 14 % |

The one thing the document asked for that this version does not do is section
1.6's benchmark restructuring in full: the tables are reordered and the HTML
and URL embeddings are added, but no sample was added to the corpus, so the
JSON-heavy composition it criticises in section 5 is unchanged.
